The paired independent-loop cases use two separate fluid circuits; the two IN SERIES cases share one closed flow path and test opposite versus synchronized moving walls. Every case includes two powered iris drives and motor losses. Total flow between these topologies is not directly comparable.

What the model actually moves

The four-bar crank and coupler are solved from mechanical link lengths at each input angle. The follower drives a membrane aperture approximation, changing actual chamber volume. Each head has a calculated suction or discharge rate from its volume derivative. In the IN SERIES experiment, two moving volumes obey separate mass conservation equations, linked by two directed ideal check valves around one closed fluid circuit. The generated flow drives hydraulic inertia and magnetic braking.

Displayed membrane strokes are visually enlarged to be legible; actual radii are in the numerical receipt. A check valve is idealized, with no unresolved reverse leakage.

90°, north/south and opposite rotation

90°: The idealized coil mutual inductance approaches zero in the chosen symmetric arrangement; this can reduce coupling rather than amplify it. North facing south / inverted: Reverses electrical polarity and changes mutual interactions, but output still requires mechanical pumping energy. Counter-rotating: The second iris crank turns backwards, altering phase and pulse timing; fluid direction is separately set by the ideal check valve.

The alternating IN SERIES pair initiates forward flow from rest in the numerical circuit; synchronized strokes provide the control. The test tracks coil magnetic energy, membrane compliance and fluid kinetic energy. No free spin-up or efficiency gain is awarded.

Model: 1D hydraulic inertance + compliance, planar four-bar closure, linear reciprocal RL mutual coupling, passive electrical resistance. No 3D CFD, field map, moving-mesh elastomer FSI, pump characterization or measured fabrication.
